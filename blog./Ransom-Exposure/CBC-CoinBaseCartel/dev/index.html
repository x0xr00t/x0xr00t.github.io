02. Who They Were

RansomHub appeared in February 2024 and within months became one of the most prolific ransomware operations in the world. Technical analysis showed strong code overlap with Knight ransomware, which itself descended from the Cyclops group — making RansomHub effectively a rebrand with better affiliate terms.

Its business model was aggressive: RansomHub raised the affiliate payout split from 90/10 to 85/15 in the affiliates' favor (January 2025), poaching experienced operators from LockBit as that brand declined. By late 2024, it had effectively supplanted LockBit as the dominant RaaS brand.

Per CISA, the core platform did not provide built-in data-exfiltration tooling — affiliates ran their own exfiltration and double-extortion playbooks. Exfiltrated victim data was also sold on the group's leak site.

03. Timeline of Rise and Collapse

  • FEB 2024
    First detections. Code analysis links the encryptor to the Knight / Cyclops lineage.
  • 2024
    Rapid affiliate growth; targets Citrix CVE-2023-3519 and other edge-device exploits; becomes one of the most active leak-site brands.
  • JUL–DEC 2024
    Affiliates deploy EDRKillShifter (documented by Sophos) using vulnerable drivers such as RentDrv2 and ThreatFireMonitor.
  • 24 JAN 2025
    Affiliate split raised to 85/15 — a deliberate talent raid on orphaned LockBit operators.
  • 31 MAR 2025
    Leak site goes dark. Affiliate communication portal goes offline (~1 Apr 2025).
  • 1–8 APR 2025
    DragonForce claims takeover of RansomHub's infrastructure, publicly recognized on 8 April 2025. A new ransomware build and a DDoS feature appear in its affiliate panel.
  • Q2 2025 → 2026
    Orphaned affiliates migrate — most visibly to Qilin, whose monthly victim count nearly doubles from ~35 to ~70. Check Point treats DragonForce's "RansomHub moved to us" claim as unverified.

05. Why It Collapsed

The precise circumstances of RansomHub's April 2025 shutdown remain unclear — Check Point notes the impact on the ecosystem was immediate, but no law-enforcement claim of responsibility was made. Competing explanations include internal infrastructure failure, an exit scam, or pressure from rival actors. What is certain:

  • The leak site and negotiation portal went dark simultaneously on 31 Mar – 1 Apr 2025.
  • DragonForce seized the moment, claiming control of RansomHub's infrastructure and advertising its panel to orphaned affiliates.
  • Affiliates — the real assets in any RaaS operation — largely defecting to Qilin, which became the dominant brand from Q2 2025 onward.
Kaspersky's 2026 ransomware review calls RansomHub the textbook case of RaaS volatility: a rapid rise, an equally rapid disappearance, and a vacuum that reshaped affiliate distribution across the entire ecosystem.

06. Defensive Takeaways

  • Brand death ≠ threat death. Affiliates survive rebrands. Track behavior and tooling (EDRKillShifter, Golang encryptors, SocGholish chains), not just leak-site names.
  • Edge devices are the front door. Patch internet-facing VPN/gateway appliances on emergency timelines.
  • Watch your drivers. BYOVD detection (vulnerable driver load by unsigned processes) catches EDR-killer tradecraft.
  • Lock down GPO. Monitor Group Policy object modifications and gpupdate execution on servers.
  • Assume double extortion. Backups defeat encryption, not exfiltration — egress monitoring matters equally.

Fourteen months. Five hundred victims. One silent collapse. RansomHub is proof that in the RaaS economy, the brand is disposable — the affiliates, the tooling and the tradecraft just migrate to the next highest bidder.

Stay curious. Stay sharp. Patch your edge. And never assume a ransomware brand is dead just because its leak site is.
